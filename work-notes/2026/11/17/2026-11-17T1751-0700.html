What was done

  • ^report(54), for the meeting: the plan brought up to date, and "Questions for the team", each with our lean (15 at 08:53, 17 by 12:43).
  • ^ticket(767), step counts and probes, Cyrus's question of 11-13. Yes in principle to both: compiled code runs in the interpreter's order, so it can add up the same steps, and with them make the same probe samples. A calibration printed the interpreter's counts for 57 small programs: a literal is one step, and each level of simple recursion 16 more. Nothing built yet.
  • ^ticket(761), the last limits: an unlabeled entry (_=1), from_lvs, and a module member that is an expression. Now 260 of 313 compile, 233 agree, and none differ.
  • ^ticket(766), mixed mode, a first step. Top-level functions with their whole types declared are compiled. The interpreter calls each as a builtin made at run time, under a budget, and a call that stops falls back to the Hazel function. Single runs in CI, interpreter alone against mixed: fib(16) 71.5 ms against 0.2 ms, and 200 I Ching readings 1,033 against 240 ms. For 500 calls of a one-line function, 22 against 27 ms, since crossing over costs more than the work.
  • ^ticket(756), the programs timed, in Node on GitHub's machines. Each repeats until its runs add up to 20 ms interpreted, or 5 ms compiled. On build 49, the 233 that agree took 32 ms a run in all interpreted, against 0.18 ms compiled. The slides' own programs joined the check: 15 of 129 compile, and all agree. To Matthew's question: of the 313, 53 are never compiled (15 holes, 10 type holes, 24 deliberately unbound names, 4 limits), and 27 are ill-typed, with no value to compare.
  • Build 49 (0e56bdbd0a), the morning's branches merged. After all of CI passed, trial/694-view-jit fast-forwarded to it at 12:15. The Witch's view took 6.8 ms a frame (about 147 fps).
  • ^ticket(761), the slide builtins. The operator builtins and to_lvs were added. Twice the full tests ran past 100 minutes; with timing left to the quick tests, they finished in 77. A real compiler bug turned up: two b2t2 table slides project a column through a list of rows, which was compiled as a plain property read. Fixed (bd08bb0872), and all 20 compiled slides agree.
  • ^ticket(768), an astrology oracle, its rules in Hazel: signs, aspects, the chart (Meeus), the sky (from astronomia, MIT, with Schlyter's terms for the outer planets), the reading and the wheel. Against Astronomy Engine, every body is within 0.11° from 1900 to 2100. Live as hazeljs-oracles 0.0.15 at 12:26. Its default chart is the app's own birth, its first commit, with the birthplace given by GPS coordinates only, as Matthew asked.
  • Two Hazel issues, filed on his word. #2831: a float literal with more than six decimals sends a module to the slow parse, which runs out of memory; writing them as ratios took the build from 70 s to 1.5 s. #2832: Evaluator.finish copies the environment into an answer that is a function, 656 MB for one, past 3 GB for another.
  • The astrology rules on hazel-jit, after four runs that timed out: one reading in CI parsed in 592 ms, elaborated in 196, ran in the interpreter in 437, compiled in 13, and ran compiled in 2.57 ms, with the same answer.
  • ^ticket(732), the Tarot's woodcut faces (CC0 1.0), from a fork kept as a submodule, in black and white or in color (0.0.14).
  • ^ticket(765), one button switches every listing on a page between highlighted text and Hazel's editor. Matthew published it as hazeljs 0.0.8.
  • ^ticket(769), John's project, in ^report(55), "Holes Across the Boundary": let a value with a hole cross into compiled code and out again unopened. No code with a hole is compiled, as agreed on 11-13. Four questions are to settle first. It also says where hazel-jit stands, for any newcomer.
